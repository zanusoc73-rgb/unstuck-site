Printer says "offline" even though it's plugged in and turned on

Applies to Windows 10/11 and macOS, most brands (HP, Canon, Epson, Brother).

This almost never means the printer is actually offline. It means your computer's print queue has decided the printer is offline, usually because of one of three things: a stale IP address, two competing printer drivers, or a spooler that's stuck. Go through these in order — most people are fixed by step 2 or 3.

Diagnose it

  1. Check it's not a real connection problem first. Print a test page directly from the printer's own menu (no computer involved). If that fails, it's a hardware or network issue, not software — check the printer's own Wi-Fi or cable connection.
  2. Clear the print queue. Open the queue for your printer and cancel every pending job, including ones that look stuck. A single jammed job is the single most common cause of "offline."
  3. Restart the print spooler (Windows) or reset the printing system (Mac). On Windows, open Services, find "Print Spooler," and restart it. On Mac, open Printers & Scanners, right-click the printer list, and choose "Reset printing system." This clears corrupted queue data that a normal cancel doesn't touch.
  4. Remove the printer and add it again. Delete the printer entirely from your device, then re-add it by searching for it fresh. This forces your computer to pull a current IP address instead of reusing a stale one — the actual root cause in most Wi-Fi printer cases.
  5. Set a static IP for the printer. If it keeps happening every few days, your router is reassigning the printer a new IP address each time it reconnects. Reserve a fixed IP for it in your router's settings (usually under "DHCP reservation") so your computer never loses track of it again.
most common fix

For about 7 in 10 people, step 4 — removing and re-adding the printer — is what actually fixes it. Steps 2 and 3 clear the symptom temporarily; step 4 clears the cause.

If it keeps coming back

Recurring offline errors (same printer, every few days) are almost always the IP address drifting, described in step 5. It's a five-minute router setting and it's permanent once set.
